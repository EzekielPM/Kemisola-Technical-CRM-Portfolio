04CRM functions built from the ground up
01 | Selected impact

Most of my best work started with a blank page.

Much of my career has started at the same point: a business knows CRM matters, but the structure is not there yet. I usually begin by listening to how the customer journey really works, finding where information or ownership breaks down, and bringing Product, Engineering, Risk, Payments and Operations into the same conversation. From there, I turn the plan into journeys, triggers, rules and working routines the team can actually run. These are some of the moments that shaped how I work today.

01GinjaBet

Turning a new CRM function into a working retention engine

I joined with no established end-to-end CRM structure to inherit. The job was to build one that made sense for the business and for the players using the product every day.

OutcomeCRM became a dependable working function with clearer journeys, stronger operating discipline and a retention model the wider business could work around.

Where I started
There was no established lifecycle operating model across onboarding, activation, retention, reactivation and churn prevention.
What I built
I put the lifecycle strategy, behavioural segments, multi-channel journeys, bonus logic, CRM QA and performance reporting in place.
How it worked
Product, Engineering, Risk, Payments and Player Operations were aligned around the same retention approach, so CRM was not working in isolation.
02betBonanza

Learning what CRM needs when the operation gets bigger

At betBonanza, the challenge changed from simply building the function to making it dependable at scale. I worked across automated acquisition, retention, churn prevention and reactivation journeys, while adding VIP treatment, loyalty mechanics, testing and risk-aware bonus controls.

The lesson was simple. Scale does not come from sending more campaigns. It comes from clearer rules, better automation and knowing when a customer should, or should not, receive an offer.

03Technical CRM architecture

Connecting a customer action to the next useful response

My technical CRM work sits between customer behaviour and platform execution. A deposit, a bet, a win, a loss or a period of inactivity should not just become another data point. It should become useful context that helps the business decide what happens next.

I work with Product, Engineering, Data, Risk and Payments to define the events, rules, integrations and controls that make those journeys reliable.

05 | About Kemisola

Strategy meets technology

I did not set out to become a Technical CRM leader. I kept following the customer problem.

My career started in digital marketing. I learned how campaigns bring people through the door, but the part that kept pulling me in was what happened next. Why did one customer stay, while another disappeared after the first interaction?

That question changed the direction of my work. I moved deeper into lifecycle, retention and the technical systems behind both. Since 2019, I have helped build four CRM functions from the ground up across startup and growth-stage iGaming businesses. Each one taught me something different about customer behaviour, technical constraints, team alignment and what it takes to make CRM useful beyond campaign delivery.

Today, I work across strategy and execution. I design journeys, rules, workflows and operating models, then work with Product, Engineering, Data, Risk, Payments, Compliance and Customer Operations to make sure they work in the real world. I enjoy fast-moving environments because they force clarity. The system has to make sense, the customer experience has to make sense and the commercial outcome has to make sense too.

Beyond the role

I lead the Nextwork Lagos community, where I mentor aspiring technology professionals and share practical lessons on cloud computing, career development and technical growth. Teaching has made me better at breaking complex ideas into something people can actually use.
