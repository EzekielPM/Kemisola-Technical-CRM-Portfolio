04CRM functions built from the ground up
01 | Selected impact

Most of my best work started with a blank page.

I have never walked into a finished CRM department and simply carried on from where someone else stopped. Each time, I had to start with the questions, the gaps and the customer problems in front of me. I learned by building, testing, fixing what did not work and turning those lessons into a CRM function the wider business could depend on. This is now the fourth time I am building a CRM function from the ground up, and each one has taught me something different.

01GinjaBet

Turning a new CRM function into a working retention engine

I joined with no established end-to-end CRM structure to inherit. The job was to build one that made sense for the business and for the players using the product every day.

OutcomeCRM became a dependable working function with clearer journeys, stronger operating discipline and a retention model the wider business could work around.

Where I started
There was no established lifecycle operating model across onboarding, activation, retention, reactivation and churn prevention.
What I built
I put the lifecycle strategy, behavioural segments, multi-channel journeys, bonus logic, CRM QA and performance reporting in place.
How it worked
Product, Engineering, Risk, Payments and Player Operations were aligned around the same retention approach, so CRM was not working in isolation.
02betBonanza

Learning what CRM needs when the operation gets bigger

At betBonanza, the challenge changed from simply building the function to making it dependable at scale. I worked across automated acquisition, retention, churn prevention and reactivation journeys, while adding VIP treatment, loyalty mechanics, testing and risk-aware bonus controls.

The lesson was simple. Scale does not come from sending more campaigns. It comes from clearer rules, better automation and knowing when a customer should, or should not, receive an offer.

03Technical CRM architecture

Why I went deeper into the technical side of CRM

Working with different CRM tools exposed me to real problems. Some triggers did not fire when expected. Some segments did not behave the way the campaign needed. Some automations looked simple on paper but became difficult once the data and backend systems were involved.

I did not want to stop at saying, "the CRM tool is not working." I wanted to understand enough to have better conversations with Engineering, Integration and backend teams. That is what pushed me into cloud computing and cloud engineering. It gave me the technical confidence to explain the customer problem clearly, understand the system behind it and help the right people get to a solution faster.

05 | About Kemisola

My CRM journey

I kept asking why we worked so hard to acquire customers without thinking enough about how to keep them.

I started my career as a Digital Marketing Strategist. Like most marketing teams, we spent time, effort and money bringing customers in. I became curious about the part that came next. We had acquisition plans, but I could not see the same level of thinking around retention. That curiosity became the reason I moved into CRM in 2019.

Since then, I have never inherited a ready-made CRM department. Every role has asked me to build something from the ground up. I had to understand the customers, create the journeys, shape the processes, work through mistakes and learn what the business really needed. Some ideas worked. Some did not. The important part was learning why, fixing the gaps and making the next version better.

The technical side came from another problem I kept meeting. Different CRM tools had their own limits. I ran into trigger issues, segmentation gaps and automations that did not behave the way the team expected. I wanted to do more than report the problem back to Engineering. I wanted to understand the conversation well enough to explain what was happening, ask better questions and work with Integration and backend teams toward a real fix.

That is why I moved into cloud computing and cloud engineering. It did not replace my CRM work. It made me better at it. I can still think about the customer, the message and the business goal, but I can also sit with technical teams and understand more of what needs to happen behind the scenes.

For the fourth time, I am building a CRM department from scratch. That is the story behind this portfolio. I am still learning, still testing and still solving the same kind of question that started the journey: how do we build a better reason for customers to stay?
